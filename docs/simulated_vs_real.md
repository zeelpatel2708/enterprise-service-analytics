# Simulated vs Real – Edition A compared with Edition B

_Generated by `src/compare_editions.py` on 2026-10-22. Both editions cover 2024-01-01 to 2026-09-30. Resolution is measured in whole calendar days for both, because the Calgary data has no times._

## Why two editions?

* **Edition A (simulated):** a full business with revenue, technicians, CSAT and targets. The data problems and business stories were planted, so I can *prove* the pipeline and dashboard find the right answers.
* **Edition B (real):** City of Calgary 311 service requests. Nobody planted anything – the defects and insights are genuine.
* **Together:** the same pipeline, model and KPI definitions run on both. The comparison shows where the simulation is realistic, where it isn't, and what each dataset can and cannot answer.

## Observations

* The real data has a much longer tail (P90 29 days vs 4). Real backlogs contain slow capital or seasonal work that a contracted-SLA business would not tolerate.
* Seasonal patterns correlate at 0.42. Both peak around weather extremes, but the drivers differ (equipment failures vs snow, waste and parks).
* The real data needed different cleaning rules: repeated ticket IDs were *updates*, not errors, and closed tickets without a closed date could be rescued from the update date. Designed defects are cleaner than real ones.

## Interview takeaway

> "I built and tested the pipeline on simulated data where I knew the right answers, then ran the same pipeline on real City of Calgary data. The comparison showed which of my assumptions held – seasonality and backlog behaviour – and which didn't, like same-day closures and the long tail of resolution times. It also showed which business questions real public data simply cannot answer, such as revenue or first-time fix."
